How it works

You build the kit, join the units together, choose what each one becomes and play. There's no soldering, and you only need a computer for updates.

Build it

Units and the Root come as kits: the circuit board already made and tested, with its top panel, base and fixings, a screwdriver and a spanner. Building one is quick: panel on, screws and posts, the screen stuck down and its cable clipped in, base on.

The magnets are already in the contacts on each side, and every part fits only one way.

A step-by-step build guide with photos will be here.

Join it

The Root goes on the left; units slide on to its right and the magnets pull them home. Up to eight units, in any order, two or more of a kind if you like. They come apart and go back together whenever you want, even while you play, and each one remembers everything: its sound, its page, its settings. Take a unit away to work on a sound, then snap it back on for the song.

Each unit gets its own MIDI channel and its own name (GLASS 1, GLASS 2...) automatically, so two of a kind just work.

Choose what it becomes

A new unit is blank. Turn its first encoder to choose one of the ten, hold the fourth, and the Root gives it its firmware. Want something else later? Choose again from SETUP. What you made on the old one stays on the Root, ready for when you change back.

Before choosing, a new unit can test itself: every button, light, colour and encoder, the sound, and the joins between units.

Keep it up to date

The Root keeps the firmware for every kind of unit, and it's the only part you update from a computer. Download one file from this website, take the units off the Root and plug it into the computer on its own. It shows up as a drive called TESSEL; drag the file onto it, and when the drive comes back, it's done. Snap the units back on and each one offers to catch up. Your sounds are kept.

With units attached, the Root is simply a MIDI device to your computer and your music software, so there's never a drive to eject when you're playing.

Play

Press SOUNDS for the factory sounds, then turn anything: every change is heard at once, and a factory sound is never overwritten. Step plays the whole row; Haze adds the room; Pilot puts the knobs you need for a live set on one unit. Or play it all from a MIDI keyboard or a computer.
